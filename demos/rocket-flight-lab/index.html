What the model calculates

A single stage starts at rest on a level launch pad. Thrust and mass flow remain constant during the burn. Fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) integrates altitude and vertical velocity at 0.02-second intervals; a step ends exactly at burnout.

m(t) = m_dry + m_prop × max(0, 1 − t / t_burn)
ρ(h) = 1.225 × exp(−h / 8500) kg/m³
dh/dt = v
dv/dt = [T − ½ρ Cd A v|v|] / m − g
g = 9.80665 m/s²

Drag opposes motion on ascent and descent. Pad contact prevents downward movement before liftoff. Apogee and ground crossings are interpolated between samples.

Limits: constant gravity, a simple exponential atmosphere, fixed drag coefficient, no wind, no staging, no attitude dynamics, no parachute, no orbital motion, no optimization, and no real-flight calibration. Numerical checks do not establish real-world prediction accuracy.

Reference: NASA Glenn · Ideal rocket equation.
